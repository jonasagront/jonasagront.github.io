High-Power Rocket

Designed, simulated, built, and flew a single-stage high-power rocket on an AeroTech H135W and recovered it intact.

Design approach

The aim was a vehicle simple enough to build reliably for a first high-power flight, and analyzed closely enough to trust before launch. I sized the airframe around a 29 mm motor mount and used OpenRocket to place the fins so the center of pressure sits well aft of the center of gravity.

The final design has a simulated static margin of 1.91 calibers, inside the commonly used 1–2 caliber range for a stable flight. A mass component in the nose cone trims the CG forward.

I then modeled the airframe in SolidWorks and exported DXF profiles for the laser-cut plywood fins and centering rings, so the fin geometry flown matched the geometry analyzed.

Independent trajectory model in RocketPy

As a second, independent prediction, I built a separate flight simulation in Python with RocketPy. Its inputs were the published H135W thrust curve, the vehicle geometry and mass properties, and historical weather for the launch site at launch time (91.4 °F, 1.3 mph surface wind).

Drag came from the OpenRocket export rather than a default curve. From 829 exported records I kept only ascent samples at angle of attack of 5° or less, split them at motor burnout (2.07 s), and built separate power-on (76 samples) and power-off (166 samples) Cd-versus-Mach curves. Cd ranged from 0.55 to 0.65.

I used the predicted trajectory to check parachute deployment timing. With the 8-second delay, ejection is predicted 0.76 s before apogee while the rocket is climbing at only about 29 mph, a low-speed deployment that keeps loads on the shock cord and nose cone small.

Problem

The rover had to stay on the bottom of the pool and drive along it under its own power. That makes buoyancy the central design problem: a sealed hull full of air wants to float, but a rover that is too heavy loses speed and is hard to handle.

The hull is a water jug, and the drive and components were packaged inside it in CAD before building.

Approach

  • Propulsion: designed a rear-mounted propulsion system for the water-jug hull, with twin motors at the stern.
  • Ballast: tuned fishing-weight ballast to balance buoyancy against weight, keeping the rover in contact with the pool floor.
  • Result: placed 26th of 481 teams, in the top 5% of the field.

Autonomous Duck Launcher

A line-following Arduino robot that drives the course, detects the wall, and fires a lever-arm launcher at the target.

RoleProgramming & launcher tuning
TimelineOct. – Dec. 2025
ResultHighest score in section, 40+ designs
ToolsArduino, C++, CAD

Control logic

I programmed the robot in C++ on Arduino. Line-following logic keeps the robot on the course line, and bump-sensor logic detects the wall at the end of the run and hands control to the launch routine.

The launch itself runs as a double-strike sequence, striking the lever arm twice in a timed pattern.

Tuning through testing

Launch distance depends on the arm's lever ratio and on strike timing. I tuned both through repeated test runs on the course to improve launch performance.
